Privacy Policy

Reset for Mac. Effective 14 October 2026.

In short

  • Reset has no account, no sign-in, no advertising, and no tracking.
  • Everything it knows about your day stays on your Mac.
  • It never records which apps you use, what you type, or what is on your screen, and it never uses the camera or the microphone.
  • Cloud personalization is optional and off until you turn it on. Even then it sends only a small summary of rounded numbers, never your name, apps, or words.
  • You can delete everything from Reset's Settings at any time.

What stays on your Mac

Reset keeps a small record of your working day so it can time your breaks and show you Today and History. It is stored in Reset's own folder on your Mac and is never sent anywhere.

WhatKept for
Active and inactive time, in segments30 days, then only as daily totals
Daily totals: active minutes, breaks suggested, taken, and skipped, natural rests, and each day's note365 days
Each break Reset suggested and what became of it, focus sessions, and which routine you did30 days
What Reset decided for you: when it held a break back and which kind of signal held it, as times and codes, never a name30 days
Your preferences: mode, rhythms, routines, working hours, protected apps, which calendars count, and themeUntil you change or delete them
A small snapshot for the desktop widget: Reset's state, the next break, and today's countsReplaced at each change

Reset never records which app or window you use, what you type, what is on your screen, your name, your email, or your location.

Signals Reset reads and does not keep

To avoid interrupting you at a bad moment, Reset asks macOS a few yes-or-no questions. The answers are used in memory only, never sent anywhere, and never written down, except for the fact that a break was held and for how long.

  • Microphone in use. Whether another app is using an audio input. Reset never uses the camera or the microphone, opens no audio stream, and asks for no Microphone permission. It reads no app or device name.
  • Display kept awake. Whether another app is keeping the display awake, as during a video or a presentation. Reset reads nothing on your screen.
  • Fullscreen. The size and position of the front window, from the list macOS keeps. Reset never reads window titles or contents, and asks for no Accessibility permission.
  • A pause in typing. How many seconds ago a key was last pressed or the pointer last dragged. Reset never learns which key was pressed, how many, or how fast.
  • Calendar, if you allow it. The start and end of events on the calendars you choose, whether each is busy or free, and your own reply. Never an event's title, notes, location, or attendees. Reset never changes your calendar.
  • Focus filters and Shortcuts. A Focus you add Reset to can tell it to hold breaks or use a mode. Shortcuts actions do what Reset's own buttons do. Nothing about them is sent anywhere.

Permissions, all optional

  • Notifications let Reset remind you gently. Without them, the menu-bar timer and break screens still work.
  • Calendar is asked only when you click Enable in Settings. Without it, everything else works.
  • Open at login is a normal macOS login item you control in Settings.

Cloud personalization

Cloud personalization is optional and part of Reset Pro. It is off until you turn it on in Settings, Personalization & privacy, which lists what is sent before you choose. Nothing is sent, and no key is created, until then.

When it is on, Reset asks its own service to rank the routines Reset already has and to pick which of your day's facts are worth showing. The service uses Gemini Flash on Google Cloud Vertex AI.

What is sent

  • Version numbers of the app's routines and rules, and your language code.
  • Your active minutes over the last seven days, rounded to five minutes, split into morning, afternoon, and evening.
  • How many breaks you completed or skipped, by kind, for example "eyes: 3".
  • Which routines are available to you, your break settings, and whether you prefer seated routines.
  • A few rounded numbers for the daily note, such as breaks taken of breaks suggested.
  • A random request ID.

Nothing else: no times of individual actions, no app names, no free text. The model can only choose among routines the app sent, and the words you see are written on your Mac. Requests are limited to a few a day and are never made during a break. If the service cannot be reached, Reset uses its own rotation.

No account, even here

Turning personalization on creates a private key on your Mac, in the Secure Enclave where your Mac has one, and registers its public half with the service. The service knows your installation only by a hash of that key. No name, email, or device serial is involved.

What the service keeps

  • Your registration, under that hash, until 30 days pass without use or until you delete it.
  • Rate-limit records, which expire within three days.
  • A one-way hash of each request for 24 hours, to recognise repeats. It cannot be turned back into your numbers.

The service stores none of the numbers above and none of the model's answers. Its logs record route names, status codes, timings, and token counts, never request contents. Google Cloud's own request logs may record your IP address for a limited time; Reset does not read or use them. Google's Vertex AI terms state that customer prompts are not used to train Google's models.

Purchases

Reset Pro is sold through the Mac App Store. Apple handles payment, trials, renewals, and refunds; Reset never sees your payment details. The app checks with Apple, on your Mac, whether a purchase is active. Nothing about your purchase is sent to Reset's service, which treats every installation the same way.

Deleting your data

  • Delete history, in Settings, Personalization & privacy, removes your history and keeps your settings.
  • Reset all data, in the same place, removes the local database, your preferences, Reset's Keychain items, and the widget's file, and asks the service to delete your registration. If the service cannot be reached, Reset retries later and tells you the deletion is not yet confirmed.
  • You can also delete the app and its folder, ~/Library/Containers/com.social.reset.

Children

Reset collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
